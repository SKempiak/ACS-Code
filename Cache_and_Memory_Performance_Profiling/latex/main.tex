\begin{abstract}
  We profile the cache and memory hierarchy of an Intel i9-10900F with four purpose-built
  microbenchmarks, user-space core counters, memory-controller counters and Intel MLC.
  Zero-queue pointer-chase latency is \valqty{zq_L1_ns}{\nano\second} in L1, \valqty{zq_L2_hugens}{\nano\second}
  for an L2 hit, \valqty{zq_L3_ns}{\nano\second} in L3 and \valqty{zq_DRAM_ns}{\nano\second} in DRAM with
  \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages (\valqty{zq_DRAM_hugens}{\nano\second} with \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages). A
  loaded-latency probe running beside up to nine streaming cores traces the throughput--latency
  curve: the knee is at four cores, \valqty{int_sat_gib}{\gibi\byte\per\second} (95\% of peak) at
  \val{int_sat_ratio}$\times$ the unloaded latency, matching MLC. With measured latency, Little's Law
  separates the \valnum{int_L0max} lines the controller serves from up to \valnum{int_Lmax} that queue.
  Counter-fitted AMAT explains the \valnum{cm_rand_span}$\times$ random-SAXPY slowdown, \qty{2}{\mebi\byte}
  pages remove a page-walk cost of up to \valqty{tlb_gap_65536_ns}{\nano\second} per access, and
  memory-controller counters confirm the read-for-ownership cost of writes. A cache-resident buffer
  reports \valqty{pit_small_gib}{\gibi\byte\per\second} of nonexistent ``DRAM'' bandwidth.
\end{abstract}
