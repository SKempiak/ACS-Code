\section{Working-Set Sweep}\label{sec:working-set}
\rubric{Working-set, cache and TLB analysis}{35}{Counters and performance correlated correctly (with
\cref{sec:cache-miss,sec:tlb}).}

\begin{hypothesis}
  Pointer-chase latency should trace the capacity staircase: flat while the set fits in L1
  (\qty{32}{\kibi\byte}), a step up through the \qty{256}{\kibi\byte} L2, a wider plateau across the
  \qty{20}{\mebi\byte} L3, and a final jump to DRAM latency. The counters should show the cause: L1D
  misses per access rising from 0 to 1 past \qty{32}{\kibi\byte}, LLC misses per access rising to 1 past
  \qty{20}{\mebi\byte}, and dTLB walks appearing once the set exceeds the \qty{4}{\kibi\byte}-page TLB
  reach (\ensuremath{\sim}\qty{6}{\mebi\byte}) but not with \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Fixed       & 1 thread pinned to CPU~2; one node per \qty{64}{\byte} line; user-space counters \\
  Varied      & working-set size \qty{8}{\kibi\byte}--\qty{512}{\mebi\byte}; random vs.\ sequential; \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} vs.\ \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages (random) \\
  Script      & \code{Cache_and_Memory_Performance_Profiling/scripts/run.sh working_set} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
The latency staircase and the counters line up level by level (\cref{fig:working-set}). L1D misses per
access jump from \ensuremath{\approx}0 to 1 between \qty{32}{\kibi\byte} and \qty{48}{\kibi\byte}, exactly at
the L1 capacity. With \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages, LLC references (L2 misses) already appear inside the L2 window, well
before the \qty{256}{\kibi\byte} capacity, but not monotonically: \val{ws_64k_4k_llcref} per access at
\qty{64}{\kibi\byte}, \val{ws_96k_4k_llcref} (essentially none) at \qty{96}{\kibi\byte},
\val{ws_128k_4k_llcref} at \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} and \val{ws_192k_4k_llcref} at \qty{192}{\kibi\byte}; they
approach one per access only by \qtyrange{1}{2}{\mebi\byte}. Page walks start at \qty{8}{\mebi\byte}, just
past the \ensuremath{\approx}\qty{6}{\mebi\byte} STLB reach, and explain why the \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} latency
already climbs inside the L3 region (\valqty{ws_8m_4k_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\
\valqty{ws_8m_huge_ns}{\nano\second} with \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages at \qty{8}{\mebi\byte}). LLC misses
appear from \qtyrange{12}{16}{\mebi\byte}, before the nominal \qty{20}{\mebi\byte}, and reach one per access
by \qty{64}{\mebi\byte}.

\paragraph{A page-size crossover at the L3 edge.} Near the L3 capacity the two page sizes swap order.
At \qty{12}{\mebi\byte} and \qty{16}{\mebi\byte} the \qty{2}{\mebi\byte}-page chase is \emph{slower} than the
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte}-page chase (\valqty{ws_12m_huge_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{ws_12m_4k_ns}{\nano\second},
and \valqty{ws_16m_huge_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{ws_16m_4k_ns}{\nano\second}), with more LLC misses per
access (\val{ws_12m_huge_llcmiss} vs.\ \val{ws_12m_4k_llcmiss}, and \val{ws_16m_huge_llcmiss} vs.\
\val{ws_16m_4k_llcmiss}) and no page walks at all. From \qty{24}{\mebi\byte} on, the order reverts
(\valqty{ws_24m_huge_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{ws_24m_4k_ns}{\nano\second}), and the
\qty{2}{\mebi\byte} curve is not even monotonic (\qty{16}{\mebi\byte} is slower than \qty{24}{\mebi\byte}). Page-table lines competing for
the L3 therefore cannot explain the early LLC misses: the configuration with the most early misses
has no page tables to walk. \emph{Untested hypothesis:} the cause is physical placement in the L3. The L3
is split into slices selected by a hash of the physical address, and sets are indexed by lower address bits.
A \qtyrange{12}{16}{\mebi\byte} buffer of \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages occupies only 6--8 physically contiguous
frames, so any unevenness in how those few frames map to slices and sets is shared by every line in them;
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages scatter the buffer over thousands of random frames (page colours), which averages
the occupancy out. A test would repeat the \qtyrange{12}{16}{\mebi\byte} points over several fresh huge-page
allocations (and with \code{hugetlbfs} pages) and check whether the LLC-miss rate varies from allocation to
allocation.

In DRAM the
random 4\,KiB-page chase incurs \val{ws_dram_rand4k_dtlb} completed page walks and
\val{ws_dram_rand4k_l1d} L1D misses per access (versus \val{ws_dram_randhuge_l1d} with 2\,MiB pages): the
page walker's own page-table reads miss the L1 too, which is why the 4\,KiB curve sits above the 2\,MiB curve
(\valqty{ws_dram_rand4k_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{ws_dram_randhuge_ns}{\nano\second} at
\qty{512}{\mebi\byte}). The sequential chase costs only \valqty{ws_dram_seq_ns}{\nano\second} in DRAM with
\val{ws_dram_seq_llcmiss} demand LLC misses per access versus \val{ws_dram_rand_llcmiss} for random: the
prefetcher turns its misses into hits.

Transitions are gradual rather than sharp. At \qty{192}{\kibi\byte} (75\% of L2) the 4\,KiB-page chase
already costs \valqty{ws_l2edge_4k_ns}{\nano\second} with \val{ws_l2edge_4k_llcref} L2 misses per
access, while the 2\,MiB-page chase stays at \valqty{ws_l2edge_huge_ns}{\nano\second}
(\val{ws_l2edge_huge_llcref}): almost no L2 misses. The likely explanation is page placement:
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages scatter the buffer over arbitrary physical
frames, so some L2 sets receive more lines than their associativity allows (conflict misses), whereas a
physically contiguous \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} page spreads lines evenly over the sets. The placement is
decided per allocation, which explains why the \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} L2 window is not monotonic: each size
is a fresh buffer with its own set of frames (page colours), and at \qty{96}{\kibi\byte} the frames
happened to cover the sets evenly (\valqty{ws_96k_4k_ns}{\nano\second}, the L2 hit latency, against
\valqty{ws_64k_4k_ns}{\nano\second} at \qty{64}{\kibi\byte}). Each size runs in its own process, whose
8 repetitions reuse one buffer and agree closely. A different allocation of the same size can differ even
with the identical ring (same seed): the zero-queue run of \cref{sec:zero-queue} measured \valqty{zq_64k_ns}{\nano\second} at \qty{64}{\kibi\byte} and
\valqty{zq_128k_ns}{\nano\second} at \qty{128}{\kibi\byte}, the reverse of this sweep's
\valqty{ws_64k_4k_ns}{\nano\second} and \valqty{ws_128k_4k_ns}{\nano\second}. This staircase is the
input to the AMAT model in \cref{sec:cache-miss}. Counter multiplexing was in effect for some events
(minimum running fraction \val{ws_ctr_cover_min}); counts are scaled accordingly.
