\section{TLB-Miss Impact}\label{sec:tlb}

\begin{hypothesis}
  With \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages, a dependent access to a random page should cost extra once the pages
  touched exceed the L1 dTLB (64 entries), and again past the second-level TLB (1536 entries,
  \ensuremath{\approx}\qty{6}{\mebi\byte}), where every access walks. With \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages (512$\times$
  the reach per entry) the walks and extra latency should vanish.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Chase        & one node per \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} page (node stride \qty{4160}{\byte} = page + one line, so nodes fall into different cache sets); random order; 16--65536 pages \\
  SAXPY        & random-order $y_i = a\,x_i + y_i$, $N = 2^{18}\ldots2^{24}$ \\
  Varied       & page size: \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} (\code{MADV_NOHUGEPAGE}) vs.\ \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} transparent huge pages (\code{MADV_HUGEPAGE}), both set before first touch; the chase also uses reserved \code{hugetlbfs} \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages (\code{MAP_HUGETLB}) \\
  Verification & \code{AnonHugePages} per run; dTLB-walk counter per access \\
  Script       & \code{Cache_and_Memory_Performance_Profiling/scripts/run.sh tlb} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
Page size is the only difference between the curves, so any gap is TLB cost; both TLB levels show as
separate cliffs (\cref{fig:tlb}). Up to 64 pages both page sizes cost
the same (\valqty{tlb_4k_64_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{tlb_huge_64_ns}{\nano\second}). At 128 pages the
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte} chase jumps to \valqty{tlb_4k_128_ns}{\nano\second} while the \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} chase
stays at \valqty{tlb_huge_128_ns}{\nano\second}, with no walks in either: the
\valqty{tlb_gap_128_ns}{\nano\second} (\valnum{tlb_gap_128_cyc}~cycles) gap is an L1 dTLB miss that hits the
STLB. Near the STLB capacity walks begin
(\val{tlb_4k_1536_dtlb} per access at 1536 pages), and at 2048 pages every \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} access walks
(\val{tlb_4k_2048_dtlb} walks per access): \valqty{tlb_4k_2048_ns}{\nano\second} against
\valqty{tlb_huge_2048_ns}{\nano\second}, a \valqty{tlb_gap_2048_ns}{\nano\second} (\valnum{tlb_gap_2048_cyc}-cycle)
page-walk penalty while page-table entries still hit in cache. Past 4096 pages the data footprint leaves
L2, raising both curves (huge pages: \valqty{tlb_huge_4096_ns}{\nano\second}), so only the gap is attributed
to the TLB. It grows as the page tables outgrow the caches: \valqty{tlb_gap_16384_ns}{\nano\second} at 16384 pages and
\valqty{tlb_gap_65536_ns}{\nano\second} at 65536 pages (\valqty{tlb_4k_65536_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\
\valqty{tlb_huge_65536_ns}{\nano\second}, \val{tlb_ratio_65536}$\times$), while \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages
stay at \val{tlb_huge_65536_dtlb} walks.

For random SAXPY (\cref{tab:tlb-saxpy}) at $N=2^{24}$, \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} pages cut page walks from
\val{tlbs_4k_dtlb} to \val{tlbs_huge_dtlb} per element and runtime from \valqty{tlbs_4k_ns}{\nano\second}
(\code{cache_miss} run: \valqty{cm_rand_max_ns}{\nano\second}) to
\valqty{tlbs_huge_ns}{\nano\second} per element, a \val{tlbs_speedup}$\times$ speedup, a little below the
\val{tlb_ratio_65536}$\times$ of the dependent chase: overlap hides only a small part of the walks, since each
adds dependent page-table reads before its data miss. The largest speedup is at $N=2^{20}$, where the
\qty{2}{\mebi\byte}-page data still mostly hit in the L3 but \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} pages already walk about once
per element.

\paragraph{Huge pages are verified, not assumed.} Every ``huge'' row was fully backed by
\code{AnonHugePages} (e.g.\ \qty{266240}{\kibi\byte} for the 65536-page chase, \qty{327680}{\kibi\byte} for SAXPY
at $N=2^{24}$; summarised in \code{anon_thp.txt}), every \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} row records \qty{0}{\kibi\byte},
and the walk counter confirms it. Reserved \code{hugetlbfs} pages (\code{MAP_HUGETLB}) give the same latency as THP
(\valqty{tlb_hugetlb_4096_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{tlb_huge_4096_ns}{\nano\second} at 4096 pages,
\valqty{tlb_hugetlb_65536_ns}{\nano\second} vs.\ \valqty{tlb_huge_65536_ns}{\nano\second} at 65536), with zero
walks.
