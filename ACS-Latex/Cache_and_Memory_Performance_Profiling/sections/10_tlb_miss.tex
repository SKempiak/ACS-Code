\section{TLB-Miss Impact}\label{sec:tlb}

\begin{hypothesis}
  For random access over a footprint far larger than the TLB reach ($\sim$1536 entries $\times$
  \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} $\approx$ \qty{6}{\mebi\byte} on this core), \qty{2}{\mebi\byte} huge pages should cut
  dTLB misses and page-walk cost and shave runtime, because one TLB entry then covers 512$\times$ more
  memory.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Kernel       & $y_i = a\,x_i + y_i$ (float64), random traversal \\
  Varied       & page size (base \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} vs.\ transparent huge pages); $N = 2^{18} \ldots 2^{24}$ \\
  Verification & \code{AnonHugePages} read from \code{/proc/self/smaps\_rollup} at runtime \\
  Script       & \code{Cache_and_Memory_Performance_Profiling/scripts/run.sh tlb} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
The base-page and huge-page curves are statistically identical (e.g.\ at $N=2^{24}$,
\qty{21.6}{\nano\second} vs.\ \qty{21.4}{\nano\second}). The reason is not that huge pages fail to help:
despite THP being set to \code{always}, a \qty{2}{\mebi\byte}-aligned mapping and
\code{madvise(MADV\_HUGEPAGE)}, \code{/proc/self/smaps\_rollup} reported \code{AnonHugePages: 0 kB} on
every run, so the allocation was never backed by huge pages. Short-lived processes do not give
\code{khugepaged} time to collapse pages, and this sandbox appears to withhold THP from the cgroup.
Isolating a real TLB effect would require explicit \code{hugetlbfs} (\code{MAP\_HUGETLB} with reserved
pages, which needs privileges we do not have) or \code{perf}'s \code{dTLB-load-misses} counter (not
installed). We therefore report this as a limitation rather than a result: the experiment and its
verification are in place and will show the expected gap on a host where huge pages are actually granted.
The verification hook is itself the useful outcome---it caught a silent failure that a naive benchmark
would have mistaken for ``huge pages don't help.''
