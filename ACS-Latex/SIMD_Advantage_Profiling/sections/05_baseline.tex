\section{Scalar vs.\ SIMD Baseline}\label{sec:baseline}
\rubric{Baseline, correctness, reproducibility}{15}{Speedup and achieved throughput for every kernel
across working-set sizes.}

\begin{hypothesis}
  For compute-friendly, unit-stride kernels (SAXPY, elementwise multiply, stencil) we expect the
  \code{native} speedup to approach the float32 lane count ($8\times$) while data is L1/L2-resident,
  and to fall toward $1\times$ once the working set is DRAM-resident and all variants share the same
  bandwidth. \code{autovec} (\num{4} lanes) should track at roughly half the \code{native} speedup.
  The dot product should barely speed up at any size because its reduction does not reassociate
  (\cref{sec:vectorization}).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Fixed       & float32; unit stride; aligned arrays; one thread pinned to CPU~2 \\
  Varied      & kernel; variant (scalar, autovec, native); $N = 2^{8} \ldots 2^{25}$ \\
  Repetitions & 12 per point; median, IQR error bars \\
  Script      & \code{SIMD_Advantage_Profiling/scripts/run.sh baseline} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
At $N=\num{4096}$ (L1-resident) the \code{native} speedups are SAXPY $5.79\times$
(\qty{20.2}{\giga\flop\per\second}), stencil $3.49\times$ (\qty{17.3}{\giga\flop\per\second}),
elementwise multiply $3.55\times$ and dot $1.01\times$. \code{autovec} reaches about half of
\code{native} for SAXPY ($3.02\times$) and \SIrange{70}{80}{\percent} of it for multiply and stencil
($2.54\times$, $2.79\times$). None reaches the full $8\times$ lane count even in cache: each vector
FMA still needs two \qty{256}{bit} loads and a store, and native SAXPY needs
\numrange{0.22}{0.28}~cycles per element in L1 (\cref{sec:locality}), against the
$0.125$~cycles/element (one 8-lane iteration per cycle) that two load ports and one store port
would allow. So the loop is limited by load/store issue well before it is FLOP-bound.

The kernels mostly behave as predicted, and the exceptions are informative.
\emph{Elementwise multiply} streams three arrays (\qty{12}{\byte}/element), so it leaves L1 earlier
(near $N\approx\num{2700}$) than SAXPY. Its speedup peaks at $5.54\times$ at $N=\num{2048}$ and has
already dropped to $3.55\times$ at $N=\num{4096}$. The \emph{stencil} has the highest FLOP count (5)
and reuses $x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1}$, so it stays the most compute-bound and keeps a $2.6$--$2.7\times$
advantage even in DRAM (\cref{fig:baseline-speedup}). Its native speedup is irregular, though:
$3.3$--$3.5\times$ for $N=\num{1024}\ldots\num{16384}$ but $4.5$--$6.2\times$ for
$N=\num{32768}\ldots\num{1048576}$. In other words, the cache-resident stencil runs \emph{slower} per element than the
L3-resident one (\cref{sec:anomalies}). The \emph{dot product} is the reduction case from
\cref{sec:vectorization}: packed multiplies, serial sum, $\approx1\times$ at every size, at
\qtyrange{1.33}{1.49}{\giga\flop\per\second}.

By $N=\num{16777216}$ (\qty{128}{\mebi\byte}, far past L3) every speedup has compressed: SAXPY
$1.94\times$, multiply $1.51\times$, stencil $2.72\times$, dot $1.00\times$ (GFLOP/s of every
kernel and build: \cref{tab:baseline-gflops}).
\Cref{fig:baseline-throughput} shows the asymmetry behind this. Native SAXPY falls from
\num{20.2} to \qty{4.7}{\giga\flop\per\second} on a DRAM plateau, while scalar only falls from
about \qty{3.5}{\giga\flop\per\second} in L1 to \qty{2.4}{\giga\flop\per\second} in DRAM. The two
plateaus do not merge because scalar SAXPY stays largely instruction-bound even in DRAM. It needs
\numrange{1.5}{1.8}~cycles of core work per element with all data in cache, at an IPC near 4, and
\num{2.3}~cycles per element from DRAM, still at an IPC of \num{3.0} (\cref{sec:locality}). At the
fixed \qty{2.8}{\giga\hertz} clock this persistent ratio is larger than in the turbo-clock sweep
($1.32\text{--}1.44\times$, \cref{sec:method}), as expected if scalar is core-bound and native
memory-bound. Separate sweeps agree: SAXPY at $N=\num{16777216}$ gives $1.94\times$ (baseline and
locality), $1.95\times$ (negative case), $1.99\times$ (data type) and $1.91\text{--}2.02\times$
across the alignment sweep's offsets, and $1.88$--$1.92\times$ at $N=\num{33554432}$. The curves bend downward at the L1 ($N\approx\num{4096}$) and L2
($N\approx\num{32768}$) boundaries; \cref{sec:locality} resolves those transitions and
\cref{sec:roofline} ties each plateau to a measured bandwidth roof.
