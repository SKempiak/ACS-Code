\section{Alignment and Tail Handling}\label{sec:alignment}
\rubric{Alignment, tail, stride/gather studies}{20}{Quantified alignment, prologue/epilogue and masking
effects with a convincing explanation (with \cref{sec:stride}).}

\begin{hypothesis}
  A start address that is not \qty{32}{\byte}-aligned should force the \qty{256}{bit} loads to be
  unaligned and/or trigger a scalar peel prologue, costing a modest fixed overhead that is most
  visible at small $N$. Because this is a client Skylake core, unaligned \code{vmovups} that happen
  to stay within a cache line should be nearly free, so we expect the penalty to be small
  (tens of percent) and to recur with \qty{64}{\byte} period as the offset sweeps a cache line.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Fixed       & kernel SAXPY; float32; unit stride; native build; $N=\num{4096}$ (L1-resident, to
                isolate the prologue from bandwidth) \\
  Varied      & start offset $0\ldots\qty{60}{\byte}$ (multiples of \num{4}); and $N$ stepping across
                a \num{8}-lane boundary at offset $0$ \\
  Script      & \code{SIMD_Advantage_Profiling/scripts/run.sh alignment} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
The alignment penalty is real but small. At offset $0$ and offset \qty{32}{\byte}---both
\qty{32}{\byte}-aligned, since the base is \qty{64}{\byte}-aligned---native SAXPY runs at
$\approx\qty{0.056}{\nano\second}$ per element. Every non-multiple-of-32 offset ($4,8,\ldots,28,36,60$)
costs $\approx\qty{0.080\text{--}0.090}{\nano\second}$, i.e.\ a \emph{\num{40}--\SI{60}{\percent}}
slowdown, and the pattern recurs with the \qty{32}{\byte} vector period (offset \num{32} is fast
again). The penalty comes from the compiler's aligned-access assumption: at an unaligned start it
either peels a short scalar prologue to reach alignment or issues split loads that straddle a
\qty{64}{\byte} cache line, and at $N=\num{4096}$ that fixed cost is still a measurable fraction of a
short loop.

The tail sub-figure shows the per-element time is essentially flat as $N$ steps from \num{4088} to
\num{4128} across the \num{8}-lane boundary: there is no sharp jump when $N$ stops being a multiple of
\num{8}. The disassembly (\cref{sec:vectorization}) explains it---GCC handles the remainder with a
\emph{vectorized} \qty{128}{bit} epilogue rather than a slow scalar loop, so processing the last
$1\text{--}7$ elements is cheap. There are no AVX-512 mask registers to discuss here because the CPU
has no AVX-512; masked tails would be the AVX-512 alternative to this narrowing epilogue.
