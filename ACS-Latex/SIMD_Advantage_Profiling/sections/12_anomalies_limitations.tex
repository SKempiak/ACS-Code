\section{Anomalies and Limitations}\label{sec:anomalies}
\rubric{Report quality}{10}{Clear plots, concise writing, honest anomalies and thoughtful limitations.}

\subsection{Unexpected results}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dot product never speeds up.} We expected a reduction to vectorize at $\approx8\times$
        like the other float32 kernels. Instead native dot stays at $\approx1\times$ across all sizes.
        Hypothesis: without \code{-ffast-math} the compiler may not reassociate floating-point
        addition, so it vectorizes the multiply but keeps a serial scalar sum. Tested: the
        disassembly shows \code{vmulps} on \code{ymm} followed by a chain of scalar \code{vaddss}
        (\cref{sec:vectorization}), confirming the hypothesis. We chose not to enable
        \code{-ffast-math} so that all variants remain bit-reproducible against the reference.
  \item \textbf{Native speedup slightly exceeds expectations at very small $N$.} At $N=512$ SAXPY
        reaches $6.5\times$, above the $\approx5.8\times$ seen at $N=4096$. Hypothesis: at tiny sizes
        the scalar loop's per-call and loop-overhead fraction is larger, inflating the ratio; the
        aggressive $8\times$ unrolling of the native loop also hides latency better when everything is
        L1-hot. This is a small-$N$ measurement artifact rather than a real microarchitectural effect.
  \item \textbf{Stencil keeps a $2\times$ advantage in DRAM} while SAXPY and multiply fall below
        $1.4\times$. Hypothesis: the stencil reuses $x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1}$, so its traffic per FLOP is
        lower (higher arithmetic intensity, \qty{0.42}{\flop\per\byte}); it is less bandwidth-bound and
        retains more of the vector benefit. Consistent with its position on the roofline
        (\cref{sec:roofline}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No hardware counters.} \code{perf} is not installed on the test host and
        \code{perf_event_paranoid} is \num{2}, so we could not record cache-miss, TLB or
        memory-traffic counters. Cache transitions are inferred from known cache sizes and the shape
        of the locality curve rather than measured directly; the derived memory bandwidth comes from
        kernel throughput, not from a counter.
  \item \textbf{Frequency not fixed.} The governor is \sysGovernor{} with turbo enabled, so the core
        frequency can vary with temperature and load. We mitigate this with warm-up passes, 12
        repetitions and median/IQR reporting, but absolute GFLOP/s carry a few-percent uncertainty and
        the peak-FLOP estimate in \cref{sec:roofline} assumes a representative \qty{4.6}{\giga\hertz}.
  \item \textbf{One machine, one compiler.} All results are from a single i9-10900F with GCC
        \sysGCC{}. Clang was used only to confirm the build compiles. No AVX-512 part was available,
        so the \qty{512}{bit} regime is out of scope.
  \item \textbf{THP always-on; SMT enabled.} Transparent huge pages are \code{always} and SMT is on
        (the sibling of the pinned core is left idle but not offlined). Neither is controlled for.
  \item \textbf{Report not compiled on the measurement host.} No \TeX{} installation was present on
        the machine used to generate the data; the figures were validated against the committed CSVs
        by column, and the document is built elsewhere.
\end{itemize}
