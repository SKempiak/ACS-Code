\section{Locality Sweep}\label{sec:locality}
\rubric{Locality and memory-bound analysis}{20}{Working-set transitions identified, and the compression
of SIMD gains in the memory-bound regime explained.}

\begin{hypothesis}
  For float32 SAXPY (two arrays, \qty{8}{\byte}/element) the per-element time should be flat and low
  while the working set fits in L1 ($2N\cdot\qty{4}{\byte}\le\qty{32}{\kibi\byte}$, i.e.\
  $N\lesssim\num{4096}$), step up at the L2 boundary ($N\approx\num{32768}$) and again past L3
  ($N\approx\num{2.6e6}$), and saturate in DRAM. The scalar and SIMD curves should be far apart in
  L1 and converge once DRAM bandwidth, which both share, dominates.
\end{hypothesis}

\subsection{Analysis}
The measured transitions line up with the cache sizes. While $N\lesssim\num{4096}$ (L1-resident)
native SAXPY holds $\approx\qty{0.045}{\nano\second}$ per element at a $\approx5.9\text{--}6.9\times$
speedup over scalar's $\approx\qty{0.31}{\nano\second}$. Crossing the \qty{32}{\kibi\byte} L1 near
$N=\num{6144}$ the native time jumps to $\approx\qty{0.08}{\nano\second}$ and the speedup drops to
$\approx4\times$; crossing the \qty{256}{\kibi\byte} L2 near $N=\num{49152}$ it falls to
$\approx2.9\times$, and by $N=\num{65536}$ to $\approx2.6\times$. Past L3 the native and scalar times
converge toward the same DRAM-limited $\approx\qty{0.25}{\nano\second}$ per element
(speedup $\approx1.35\times$, \cref{sec:baseline}).

The mechanism is bandwidth, not instruction count. In L1 the vector unit is fed fast enough that
eight-wide FMAs retire close to one per cycle, so SIMD approaches its lane-count advantage. Each
cache level further out delivers fewer bytes per cycle, so the vector loads stall more; once the data
lives in DRAM, every variant is limited by the \emph{same} memory channel and the extra lanes sit
idle waiting for operands. This is exactly the ``compression'' the rubric asks about: SIMD does not
get slower in DRAM, but scalar catches up because both are waiting on memory. The scalar curve itself
is nearly flat across cache levels (its \qty{0.31}{\nano\second} is limited by the scalar FMA +
load/store issue rate, not by cache bandwidth until DRAM), which is why the \emph{ratio} shrinks even
though absolute native time grows smoothly.
