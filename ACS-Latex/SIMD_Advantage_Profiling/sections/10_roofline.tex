\section{Roofline Interpretation}\label{sec:roofline}
\rubric{Roofline and quantitative insight}{20}{Correct arithmetic intensity, use of measured memory
bandwidth, and a justified compute- vs.\ memory-bound conclusion.}

\begin{hypothesis}
  All four kernels have very low arithmetic intensity (well under \qty{1}{\flop\per\byte}), so on a
  machine whose single-core bandwidth is tens of \unit{\giga\byte\per\second} and whose peak vector
  rate is over \qty{100}{\giga\flop\per\second} they should all sit on the \emph{bandwidth} slope of
  the roofline when DRAM-resident, i.e.\ be memory-bound. The stencil, with the highest intensity,
  should come closest to the compute roof.
\end{hypothesis}

Attainable performance at arithmetic intensity $I$ (FLOPs per byte of memory traffic) is bounded by
\begin{equation}
  P(I) = \min\bigl(P_{\text{peak}},\; B \cdot I\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{peak}}$ is the CPU's peak FLOP rate and $B$ the memory bandwidth \cite{williams2009roofline}.

\subsection{Analysis}
The ridge point sits at $I = P_{\text{peak}}/B \approx \num{147}/\num{45} \approx
\qty{3.3}{\flop\per\byte}$. Every kernel's intensity (\numrange{0.06}{0.42}~FLOP/byte) is an order of
magnitude below that, so all four are firmly memory-bound in DRAM---consistent with the hypothesis and
with the locality sweep (\cref{sec:locality}), where scalar and SIMD converge once the data leaves
cache. With $B\cdot I$ evaluated at $B\approx\qty{42}{\gibi\byte\per\second}$, SAXPY lands
essentially \emph{on} the bandwidth line (\num{5.6} measured vs $\approx\qty{5.6}{\giga\flop\per\second}$
predicted), confirming it is a pure bandwidth test; multiply and stencil reach about
\SI{71}{\percent} and \SI{72}{\percent} of their bandwidth ceilings
($\approx\num{2.8}$ and $\approx\qty{18.8}{\giga\flop\per\second}$), the shortfall coming from
write-allocate traffic and imperfect prefetch.

The dot product is the one kernel \emph{below} its bandwidth line (\num{2.2} vs a predicted
$\approx\qty{11}{\giga\flop\per\second}$): it is not memory-bound at all but latency-bound by the
non-reassociated reduction (\cref{sec:vectorization}), so the roofline---a throughput model---simply
does not apply to it. To move any streaming kernel toward the compute roof one would have to raise its
arithmetic intensity (e.g.\ fuse multiple passes, or block for cache so the data is re-read from L1
rather than DRAM); widening the vectors alone cannot help a kernel that is already waiting on memory.
