\section{Tail Latency}\label{sec:tail}
\rubric{Tail latency and device state}{25}{p50/p95/p99/p99.9 reporting and interpretation of queueing,
thermal and garbage-collection effects (with \cref{sec:device-state}).}

\begin{hypothesis}
  Moving from a mid queue depth to the near-knee depth should raise the median modestly but stretch the
  upper percentiles much more: deeper queues mean more requests waiting behind the occasional slow
  flash operation, so the p99/p99.9 gap over p50 should widen.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Fixed       & \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} random read; one job; \qty{6}{\second} runs, 5 repetitions; NVMe
                temperature recorded per repetition \\
  Varied      & iodepth 8 (mid), 32 and 64 (bracketing the knee of \cref{sec:queue-depth}), 128 (past
                it) \\
  Drive state & lean re-run: after the short sustained fill, the large file was deleted, the drive
                rested \qty{10}{\minute}, and a fresh \qty{4}{\gibi\byte} test file was preconditioned
                sequentially; reads cover its first \qty{2}{\gibi\byte} \\
  Script      & \code{SSD_Performance_Profiling/scripts/run.sh tail} (inside \code{rerun_clean.sh lean}) \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
On a rested drive the tail widens with queue depth roughly as hypothesised, but modestly:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item QD=8: p50~\csvq{tail}{qd=8}{lat_p50_us_median}, p99~\csvq{tail}{qd=8}{lat_p99_us_median},
        p99.9~\csvq{tail}{qd=8}{lat_p999_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second}; p99.9/p50 $=\ssdtailratio{8}{p999}\times$.
  \item QD=32: p50~\csvq{tail}{qd=32}{lat_p50_us_median}, p99~\csvq{tail}{qd=32}{lat_p99_us_median},
        p99.9~\csvq{tail}{qd=32}{lat_p999_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second}; $\ssdtailratio{32}{p999}\times$.
  \item QD=64: p50~\csvq{tail}{qd=64}{lat_p50_us_median}, p99.9~\csvq{tail}{qd=64}{lat_p999_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second};
        $\ssdtailratio{64}{p999}\times$.
  \item QD=128: p50~\csvq{tail}{qd=128}{lat_p50_us_median}, p99.9~\csvq{tail}{qd=128}{lat_p999_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second};
        $\ssdtailratio{128}{p999}\times$.
\end{itemize}
From QD=8 to 32 the median grows $2.4\times$ and p99.9 $3.0\times$; from 32 to 64, $1.6\times$ and
$2.3\times$. IOPS meanwhile barely grow past the knee
(\csvq{tail}{qd=64}{iops_median}$\to$\csvq{tail}{qd=128}{iops_median}). Past the knee every added
request waits in a queue, and a request queued behind one of the drive's occasional slow reads waits
longer. That stretches the upper percentiles more than the median. The p99/p50 ratio follows the same
trend (\ssdtailratio{8}{p99}$\times$ at QD=8 to \ssdtailratio{128}{p99}$\times$ at QD=128).

These tail runs agree with the original queue-depth read sweep wherever both measured the same depth:
\csvq{tail}{qd=32}{iops_median} vs.\ \csvq{queue_depth}{qd=32,mode=read}{iops_median} IOPS at QD=32,
and \csvq{tail}{qd=128}{iops_median} vs.\ \csvq{queue_depth}{qd=128,mode=read}{iops_median} at QD=128.
The exception is QD=8, where the rested drive is faster
(\csvq{tail}{qd=8}{iops_median} vs.\ \csvq{queue_depth}{qd=8,mode=read}{iops_median} IOPS) and has a
much lower p99 (\csvq{tail}{qd=8}{lat_p99_us_median} vs.\
\csvq{queue_depth}{qd=8,mode=read}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second}). The original sweep's p99
sat near \qty{0.5}{\milli\second} at every depth from 4 to 16. We suspect that it, too, met some
background flash work left by the earlier write experiments, but we did not test this.

\textbf{The tail depends on recent writes.} The original tail run (\code{tail/20261021-103201}) started
immediately after the queue-depth \emph{write} sweep, which wrote \ensuremath{\sim}\qty{11}{\gibi\byte}
of random \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} data, with no idle gap. At QD=32 it delivered \num{64475} IOPS, half the
rested value, at a median of \qty{385}{\micro\second}, and its p99.9/p50 ratio rose from $4.1\times$ at
QD=8 to $24\times$ at QD=128 (p99.9 \qty{16.0}{\milli\second}). Its temperature,
\qtyrange{50.9}{52.9}{\celsius}, was no higher than that of the lean tail run
(\qtyrange{39.9}{52.9}{\celsius} per-point medians), so the difference is not thermal. The two runs
differ in more than drive state, including the file size (\qty{2}{\gibi\byte} vs.\
\qty{4}{\gibi\byte}) and the time of day. The likely cause is still foreground reads competing with the
background folding and garbage collection of freshly written data. A read that arrives during a program,
erase or GC burst waits milliseconds, and deeper queues hold more requests behind each such stall. The
tail ratio is therefore a sensitive detector of background flash activity. Queueing alone keeps it at
3--6$\times$, while a recent heavy write load pushes it to $24\times$.

These are the numbers that govern provisioning for a latency-SLA service, not the mean. At QD=32 one read
in a thousand takes \csvq{tail}{qd=32}{lat_p999_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} on a rested drive, but
took \qty{2.6}{\milli\second} right after heavy writes. The knee from \cref{sec:queue-depth} is
therefore a latency decision as much as a throughput one: past it, almost no IOPS are gained and these
tails inflate fastest. Over both runs the NVMe composite temperature stayed at
\qtyrange{31.9}{61.9}{\celsius}, far below the \qty{86.85}{\celsius} warning threshold
(\cref{sec:device-state}).
