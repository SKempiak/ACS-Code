\begin{hypothesis}
  Moving from a mid queue depth to the near-knee depth should raise the median modestly but stretch the
  upper percentiles much more: deeper queues mean more requests waiting behind the occasional slow
  flash operation, so the p99/p99.9 gap over p50 should widen.
\end{hypothesis}

\subsection{Analysis}
At QD=8 the percentiles are p50\,\qty{170}{}, p95\,\qty{281}{}, p99\,\qty{460}{}, p99.9\,\qty{635}{\micro\second}
(\qty{46}{k} IOPS); at QD=32 they are p50\,\qty{255}{}, p95\,\qty{396}{}, p99\,\qty{563}{},
p99.9\,\qty{808}{\micro\second} (\qty{126}{k} IOPS). The median rises only $1.5\times$ while IOPS nearly
triples, but the whole distribution shifts up and the p99.9/p50 ratio stays around $3$--$3.7\times$: the
tail is dominated by requests that arrive while the controller is busy with a background
program/erase/GC operation, and a deeper queue makes a waiting request more likely. These tails are
mild compared with a rotational disk (no seeks), but they still matter: at QD=32 one read in a thousand
takes \ensuremath{\sim}\qty{800}{\micro\second}, \num{3}$\times$ the median, which for a latency-SLA service is the
number that governs provisioning, not the mean. This is why the knee from \cref{sec:queue-depth} is a
latency decision as much as a throughput one---running at QD=64+ buys little IOPS but inflates exactly
these tails.
