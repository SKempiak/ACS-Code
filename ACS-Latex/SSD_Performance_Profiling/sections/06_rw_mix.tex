\section{Read/Write Mix}\label{sec:rw-mix}
\rubric{Read/write and queue-depth analysis}{40}{Correct mix and queue-depth experiments, knee
identification and Little's Law reasoning (with \cref{sec:queue-depth}).}

\begin{hypothesis}
  At a fixed block size and queue depth, a mixed read/write workload should deliver \emph{fewer} total
  IOPS than either pure workload, not a weighted average: reads and writes contend for the controller and
  flash channels, and a read queued behind a slow program/erase inherits its latency. We expect pure
  writes to post the highest IOPS at QD=16 (write-buffer absorption) and the 50/50 mix to be the worst.
\end{hypothesis}

\subsection{Analysis}
Pure writes are fastest, at \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{iops_median} IOPS and
\csvq[0]{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_mean_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} mean, because at QD=16 the controller
absorbs them into its SLC/SRAM buffer. Pure reads reach \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=10}{iops_median} IOPS.
Mixing is worse than either pure case. The 70/30 mix drops to \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=7}{iops_median} IOPS
and the 50/50 mix to \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=5}{iops_median}, below even the read-only number. A write
landing mid-stream occupies a die (program, or the buffer flush it triggers), and reads mapped to that
die queue behind it. The interleaved writes also break up the write buffer's batching.

The p99 latency gives a related but not monotonic picture. It rises from
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=10}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} for pure reads to
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=7}{lat_p99_us_median} (70/30) and
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=5}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} (50/50). Pure writes break the trend: their
median p99 of \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} is the lowest of all four
mixes, but its IQR (\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_p99_us_q25}--\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_p99_us_q75}\,\si{\micro\second})
is by far the widest. A pure-write repetition either stays inside the write buffer and sees no tail, or
it meets a buffer flush and sees a read-like tail. The worst \emph{typical} tail belongs to the 50/50 mix,
not to the largest write fraction, so p99 is not monotonic in the write fraction.

The lesson: a \enquote{70\% read} workload is not \num{0.7} of the read result plus \num{0.3} of the
write result. The interaction term is large and negative, which is why the spec requires measuring the
mixes rather than interpolating them.
