\section{Read/Write Mix}\label{sec:rw-mix}
\rubric{Read/write and queue-depth analysis}{40}{Correct mix and queue-depth experiments, knee
identification and Little's Law reasoning (with \cref{sec:queue-depth}).}

\begin{hypothesis}
  At a fixed block size and queue depth, a mixed read/write workload should deliver \emph{fewer} total
  IOPS than either pure workload, not a weighted average: reads and writes contend for the controller and
  flash channels, and a read queued behind a slow program/erase inherits its latency. We expect pure
  writes to post the highest IOPS at QD=16 (write-buffer absorption) and the 50/50 mix to be the worst.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{knobs}
  Fixed       & \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} random; iodepth 16; one job; \code{O_DIRECT}; 5 repetitions of
                \qty{4}{\second} (points with writes also stop at \qty{256}{\mebi\byte}) \\
  Varied      & reads per 10 I/Os, deterministically interleaved: 10 (100\% R), 7 (70/30), 5 (50/50),
                0 (100\% W) \\
  Script      & \code{SSD_Performance_Profiling/scripts/run.sh rw_mix} \\
\end{knobs}

\subsection{Analysis}
Pure writes are fastest, at \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{iops_median} IOPS and
\csvq[0]{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_mean_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} mean, because the controller absorbs
them into its SLC/SRAM buffer; pure reads reach \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=10}{iops_median} IOPS. Mixing is worse
than either: 70/30 drops to \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=7}{iops_median} IOPS and 50/50 to
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=5}{iops_median}, below even pure reads. A write occupies a die (program, or the
buffer flush it triggers), reads mapped to that die queue behind it, and interleaving breaks up the
buffer's batching.

p99 rises from \csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=10}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} for pure reads to
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=7}{lat_p99_us_median} (70/30) and
\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=5}{lat_p99_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} (50/50), but pure writes have the lowest
median p99 and by far the widest IQR
(\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_p99_us_q25}--\csvq{rw_mix}{rwmix=0}{lat_p99_us_q75}\,\si{\micro\second}): a
repetition either stays inside the buffer or meets a flush. A \enquote{70\% read} workload is thus not a
weighted average of the pure results; mixes must be measured, not interpolated.
