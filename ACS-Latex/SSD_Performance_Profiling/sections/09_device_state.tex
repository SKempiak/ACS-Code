\section{Device State and Working Set}\label{sec:device-state}

\begin{hypothesis}
  Back-to-back sustained sequential writes should first ride the SLC/DRAM write cache (a burst), then
  settle to a lower steady-state throughput once the cache must flush to QLC in the background. Occasional
  latency spikes from garbage collection should appear in the upper tail even while mean throughput is
  steady.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}
  (Added before the large-file runs.) (i) A single sequential write far larger than the SLC cache should
  show a sharp \emph{cliff}: burst throughput near the QD=32 write rate of \cref{sec:block-size}, then a
  drop to the native QLC program rate, several times lower. (ii) After that, random \qty{4}{\kibi\byte}
  writes reach a lower steady state than in the burst, with heavier tails from garbage collection.
  (iii) A short idle period lets the controller drain the cache and recover part of the burst rate.
  (iv) Random reads should slow as the working set grows from \qty{1}{\gibi\byte} to
  \qty{60}{\gibi\byte}, because more logical-to-physical map lookups miss the controller's mapping cache.
  (v) \enquote{Sequential} reads of data written randomly should be much slower than of data written
  sequentially. (vi) Temperature should rise during sustained writes but stay below the
  \qty{86.85}{\celsius} warning threshold reported in sysfs (\code{temp1_max}).
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Analysis.} The fill ran at the SLC ingest rate,
\csvval{sustained_summary}{burst_mibps}\,MiB/s, for \csvval{sustained_summary}{burst_s}\,s and collapsed
after \csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB: four seconds at \qtyrange{13}{21}{\mebi\byte\per\second}
(median latency up to \qty{0.31}{\second}), then seven at \qtyrange{62}{68}{\mebi\byte\per\second}. The
post-cliff median, \csvval{sustained_summary}{steady_mibps}\,MiB/s
(\csvval[3]{sustained_summary}{steady_over_burst} of the burst, a \ssdburstratio$\times$ drop, far more
than the \enquote{several times} of hypothesis~(i)), rests on \qty{11}{\second} but matches the
\qty{62}{\mebi\byte\per\second} median of the original run's \num{1226}\,s post-cliff fill. That run never
settled: it alternated between \qtyrange{60}{70}{\mebi\byte\per\second} (\qty{55}{\percent} of seconds)
and \qtyrange{10}{20}{\mebi\byte\per\second} (\qty{32}{\percent}), averaging \qty{47}{\mebi\byte\per\second}
and drifting down. We take \ensuremath{\sim}\qty{60}{\mebi\byte\per\second} as the direct-to-QLC rate while
folding; the long-run steady state is only partly tested.

\textbf{How large is the cache?} We expected \ensuremath{\sim}\qty{20}{\gibi\byte}, but even on a trimmed,
idle drive the cliff came at \csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB, only \qty{0.7}{\gibi\byte} more
than the original run's \qty{3.1}{\gibi\byte}, so run order was not the main cause. This is the capacity
available \emph{in this drive state}; a plausible, untested explanation is a dynamic cache sized by
drive-wide free space (unknown here, as only the scratch partition was trimmed), common in QLC client
drives \cite{tallis2018intel660p,micron2210brief}.

The random-write phase (\qty{4}{\kibi\byte}, QD32, over the data just written) ran for eight full seconds
at \numrange{12471}{16548} IOPS (median \num{14666}) with a median per-second p99 of
\qty{36.0}{\milli\second}, about $16\times$ below the in-cache rate
(\csvq{queue_depth}{qd=32,mode=write}{iops_median} IOPS). This supports hypothesis~(ii) only in part:
eight seconds cannot establish a steady state. In a final \qty{0.7}{\second} the rate jumped to
\ensuremath{\sim}\num{205}k IOPS, as if SLC blocks had just been freed, and hit the \qty{1}{\gibi\byte}
cap, so the byte-weighted average in \cref{tab:sustained}
(\csvval{sustained_summary}{rand_steady_iops} IOPS) mixes two states.

After the \qty{60}{\second} idle gap the recovery probe wrote its whole \qty{1}{\gibi\byte} at
\csvval{sustained_summary}{recovery_mibps}\,MiB/s, the burst rate, so hypothesis~(iii) holds at this
scale. In the original run the same gap after tens of gigabytes gave no recovery (\qty{61}{} then
\qty{19}{\mebi\byte\per\second}): recovery depends on the fold backlog. This is short-term
behaviour, not the full-drive SNIA steady state \cite{snia_pts}.

\paragraph{Temperature.} The composite temperature stayed at
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{temp_min_c}--\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{temp_max_c}\,\si{\celsius} here and
\qtyrange{31.9}{61.9}{\celsius} over both runs, far below the \qty{86.85}{\celsius} warning threshold.
Throughput fell at \qty{35.9}{\celsius} and stayed low between \qty{34.9}{} and \qty{37.9}{\celsius}, so
the cliff is the cache, not thermal throttling.

backlog. Hypothesis~(iv) remains untested beyond \qty{4}{\gibi\byte}.

\subsection{Data layout: why preconditioning order matters}\label{sec:layout}
\begin{table}[!htbp]
  \centering\small

  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
    \toprule
    Read & after sequential fill & after random overwrite \\
    \midrule
    \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} seq, QD=1 (MiB/s)  & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median}
                                              & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median} \\
    \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} seq, QD=32 (MiB/s) & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=131072,qd=32}{mibps_median}
                                              & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=131072,qd=32}{mibps_median} \\
    \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} rand, QD=1 (IOPS)    & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=4096}{iops_median}
                                              & \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=4096}{iops_median} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

An SSD places data in the order it was \emph{written}, not by logical address, and \cref{tab:layout}
confirms hypothesis~(v) strongly. After the random overwrite, QD=1 sequential reads drop from
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median} to
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median}\,MiB/s ($6.4\times$), while
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte} random reads are unaffected. Our sub-prediction that QD=32 would hide the gap was
\emph{wrong}: it grows to $9.2\times$. Each fragmented \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} request becomes up to 32
scattered page reads, and the drive completes \ensuremath{\sim}\num{1830} such requests per second (about
\num{58}k page reads/s) at either depth, well below the \num{130}k random reads/s it sustains at QD=32; why
is untested. A sequential benchmark is meaningful only if the data's write history is controlled, which
is why \code{run.sh} rewrites the file before every sequential-read group.
