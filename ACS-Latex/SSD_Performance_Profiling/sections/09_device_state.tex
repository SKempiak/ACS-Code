\section{Device State and Working Set}\label{sec:device-state}

\begin{hypothesis}
  Back-to-back sustained sequential writes should first ride the SLC/DRAM write cache (a burst), then
  settle to a lower steady-state throughput once the cache must flush to QLC in the background. Occasional
  latency spikes from garbage collection should appear in the upper tail even while mean throughput is
  steady.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}
  (Added before the large-file runs.) (i) A single sequential write far larger than the SLC cache should
  show a sharp \emph{cliff}: burst throughput near the QD=32 write rate of \cref{sec:block-size}, then a
  drop to the native QLC program rate, several times lower. (ii) After that, random \qty{4}{\kibi\byte}
  writes reach a lower steady state than in the burst, with heavier tails from garbage collection.
  (iii) A short idle period lets the controller drain the cache and recover part of the burst rate.
  (iv) Random reads should slow as the working set grows from \qty{1}{\gibi\byte} to
  \qty{60}{\gibi\byte}, because more logical-to-physical map lookups miss the controller's mapping cache.
  (v) \enquote{Sequential} reads of data written randomly should be much slower than of data written
  sequentially. (vi) Temperature should rise during sustained writes but stay below the
  \qty{86.85}{\celsius} warning threshold reported in sysfs (\code{temp1_max}).
\end{hypothesis}

\paragraph{Analysis.} The fill began at the SLC ingest rate,
\csvval{sustained_summary}{burst_mibps}\,MiB/s, for \csvval{sustained_summary}{burst_s}\,s. After
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB it collapsed. Four seconds at
\qtyrange{13}{21}{\mebi\byte\per\second}, with per-second median latencies of up to
\qty{0.31}{\second} at QD32, were followed by seven seconds at
\qtyrange{62}{68}{\mebi\byte\per\second}. The stop rule then ended the fill at
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{fill_gib}\,GiB after \csvval{sustained_summary}{fill_s}\,s (average
\csvval{sustained_summary}{fill_avg_mibps}\,MiB/s). The median of all post-cliff seconds is
\csvval{sustained_summary}{steady_mibps}\,MiB/s, \csvval[3]{sustained_summary}{steady_over_burst} of
the burst. A burst \ssdburstratio$\times$ the steady rate is far more than the \enquote{several times}
of hypothesis~(i). The steady value rests on only \qty{11}{\second} of data. It matches, however, the
original run, whose \qty{60}{\gibi\byte} fill ran mostly at \qtyrange{60}{64}{\mebi\byte\per\second}
(with dips to \qtyrange{28}{46}{\mebi\byte\per\second}) for twenty minutes after its cliff (\code{sustained/20261021-105439}). We therefore take it as the rate at
which this QLC drive writes when it must program QLC directly while also folding cached data.

\textbf{How large is the cache?} Hypothesis~(i) assumed a fill \enquote{far larger than the SLC cache},
and the lean plan sized the fill on the assumption that a fresh cache would hold
\ensuremath{\sim}\qty{20}{\gibi\byte}. Instead, on a drive that had been trimmed and idle for half an
hour, the cliff came at \csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB. That is a measurement of the
SLC capacity \emph{available to this fill in this drive state}, not of a fixed property of the drive,
and it falls well short of the expectation. The original run had found a similar cliff at
\qty{3.1}{\gibi\byte}, and we had attributed that small value to the run order, since that fill
followed \ensuremath{\sim}\qty{60}{\gibi\byte} of writes with no idle and no TRIM. The lean re-run removes
those confounds and adds only \qty{0.7}{\gibi\byte}, so the run order was not the main reason. A
plausible explanation, which we did not test, is a dynamic SLC cache sized by free space, a common
design in QLC client drives. Such a controller can only borrow blocks it believes are unused. The
scratch filesystem had \qty{77}{\gibi\byte} free and was trimmed, but the rest of the drive, including a
\qty{351}{\gibi\byte} partition this study never touches, was not, so the drive-wide free space that
such a cache would scale with is unknown and may be small. Two other explanations remain open. Thirty
minutes may not have drained all folded data after the TRIM, or the 2210 may simply use a small
cache in this capacity class. Telling these apart would need fills at several known free-space levels,
or the vendor and SMART counters that need root (the lean run's SMART snapshot failed without it).
Under the free-space explanation the cache size is state dependent, and
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB describes this drive at its current fill level.

The random-write phase wrote \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} blocks at QD32 over the
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{fill_gib}\,GiB just written. For eight seconds the per-second rate
stayed at \numrange{12500}{16500} IOPS (median \num{14924}; quartiles
\csvval{sustained_summary}{rand_iops_q25}--\csvval{sustained_summary}{rand_iops_q75}), with a median
per-second p99 of \qty{35.7}{\milli\second}. That is about $16\times$ below the in-cache random-write
rate at the same depth (\csvq{queue_depth}{qd=32,mode=write}{iops_median} IOPS,
\cref{sec:queue-depth}), and it supports hypothesis~(ii). In the final \qty{0.7}{\second} the rate
jumped to \ensuremath{\sim}\num{205}k IOPS with a p99 of \qty{149}{\micro\second}, as if the controller
had just freed SLC blocks in the background. That burst wrote \qty{56}{\percent} of the phase's
\qty{1}{\gibi\byte} cap, which ended the phase after \qty{8.7}{\second}. The byte-weighted phase average
in \cref{tab:sustained}, \csvval{sustained_summary}{rand_steady_iops} IOPS, therefore mixes two states
and should not be read as a steady state. The per-second median is the better summary.

After the \qty{60}{\second} idle, the recovery probe wrote its whole \qty{1}{\gibi\byte} at
\csvval{sustained_summary}{recovery_mibps}\,MiB/s, the full burst rate, in under a second. At least
\qty{1}{\gibi\byte} of cache was therefore free again, and hypothesis~(iii) holds at this scale. The cap
means we cannot say how much of the \csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{cliff_gib}\,GiB came back. This
reverses the original run, where the same idle followed tens of gigabytes of writes and gave no
recovery (\qty{61}{} then \qty{19}{\mebi\byte\per\second}). Recovery depends on the size of the fold
backlog, not on the idle time alone.

Two caveats. First, because the cliff came early, the lean fill wrote only
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{fill_gib}\,GiB of a \qty{512}{\giga\byte} drive, and its post-cliff and
random phases last seconds rather than minutes. This is the short-term behaviour after the cache fills,
not the SNIA full-drive steady state, which would need the raw device (ruled out by the data-safety
rule). Second, a TRIM only marks blocks free; it does not make the drive fresh, and the state of the
untrimmed rest of the drive is unknown.

\paragraph{Temperature.} The composite temperature ranged from
\csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{temp_min_c} to \csvval[1]{sustained_summary}{temp_max_c}\,\si{\celsius}
during this experiment, and from \qtyrange{31.9}{61.9}{\celsius} over both runs (the peak was during
the QD=32 sequential-read sweep of the original run). Both are far below the
\qty{86.85}{\celsius} warning and \qty{89.85}{\celsius} critical thresholds reported by the drive. The
throughput fell within one second at \qty{35.9}{\celsius} and stayed low while the temperature moved
only between \qty{34.9}{} and \qty{37.9}{\celsius}, so the drop comes from the cache, not from thermal
throttling. In the original run the same cliff came at \qty{50.9}{\celsius}.

within \qty{1}{\percent}. The absolute read latency is about \qty{6}{\percent} lower than the original
run's clean baseline on the \qty{2}{\gibi\byte} file
(\csvq[1]{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=4096}{lat_mean_us_median}\,\si{\micro\second} QD=1
random read in \code{layout}).

Hypothesis~(iv) predicted slower random reads as the span grows from 1 to \qty{60}{\gibi\byte},
because map lookups would miss the controller's mapping cache more often. The lean re-run covers only
1--\qty{4}{\gibi\byte}. At 4 bytes of map per \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} page, \qty{4}{\gibi\byte} needs only
\qty{4}{\mebi\byte} of map, so the flat result is consistent with a mapping cache that covers at least that
much, and says nothing about larger spans. The original run did sweep 1 to \qty{60}{\gibi\byte}
(\code{working_set/20261021-105439}). There, QD=1 latency rose by about \qty{37}{\percent} and QD=32
read IOPS fell by \qty{25}{\percent}. That sweep ran right after the \qty{60}{\gibi\byte} fill, with
tens of gigabytes still being folded, in increasing span order only, and even its \qty{1}{\gibi\byte}
point reached only \num{12100} QD=1 IOPS against \num{20000} here. Its trend cannot be separated from
the fold backlog and elapsed time, so hypothesis~(iv) remains untested beyond \qty{4}{\gibi\byte}. We
could not tell whether the drive uses a host memory buffer (no allocation message in the kernel log).

An early pilot run, since discarded, exposed this effect by accident: a \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} QD=1
sequential read run right after a random-write test was several times slower than the block-size sweep.
An SSD places data in the order it was \emph{written}, not by logical address. \Cref{tab:layout} tests
this directly, and the hypothesis is confirmed strongly. After the random overwrite, QD=1 sequential
reads drop from \csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median} to
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=131072,qd=1}{mibps_median}\,MiB/s ($6.4\times$).
\qty{4}{\kibi\byte} random reads are unaffected
(\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=4096}{iops_median} vs.\
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=4096}{iops_median} IOPS), since a single page costs
the same wherever it sits.

Our sub-prediction that QD=32 would hide the gap was \emph{wrong}. At QD=32 the fragmented file gives
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_rand_overwritten,bs=131072,qd=32}{mibps_median}\,MiB/s against
\csvq{layout}{experiment=layout_seq_filled,bs=131072,qd=32}{mibps_median}, a $9.2\times$ gap, and no
better than at QD=1. Each fragmented \qty{128}{\kibi\byte} request becomes up to 32 scattered page
reads. The drive completes \ensuremath{\sim}\num{1830} such requests per second (about \num{58}k page
reads/s) at either depth, well below the \num{130}k random \qty{4}{\kibi\byte} reads/s it sustains at
QD=32. We hypothesise that the controller serializes the fragments of one command, or that read-ahead
actively wastes bandwidth on the wrong pages, but we did not test this.

This is the \enquote{insufficient preconditioning} pitfall in a subtler form. A sequential benchmark is
meaningful only if the data's write history is controlled. That is why \code{run.sh} rewrites the test
file sequentially before every sequential-read group. One group missed this in the original run:
in \code{all}, \code{fio_check} followed \code{layout} without a rewrite, so its sequential points
measured the fragmented layout (\cref{sec:fio-check}). The script has since been fixed.
